      How the simulator works
      
    
      Each process is a steady-flow device, a duct with a heating coil, a cooling coil or a humidifier. The simulator writes mass and energy balances per kilogram of dry air, because the dry-air flow is the same at the inlet and the exit while the water-vapor flow is not. Moist air is treated as an ideal-gas mixture of dry air and water vapor (the Dalton model), and its properties come from the ASHRAE Fundamentals equations coded in Psychrometricsnew.m. Notation: W is the humidity ratio (ω in the textbook), φ the relative humidity and h the enthalpy per kilogram of dry air.

      Process equations (per kilogram of dry air)
      
    
      
      
      Sensible heating or coolingNo water is added or removed; the state moves horizontally on the chart.
      
      
      Cooling with dehumidificationBelow the dew point the air leaves saturated; the condensate leaves as liquid at T2.
      
      
      Real cooling coilThe leaving air is a mixture of air brought to the coil-surface state s (saturated at Ts) and air that bypassed the coil, so it lies on the straight line from 1 to s.
      
      
      HumidificationAdiabatic. Steam: hw = hg(Tsteam); liquid water: hw = hf(Twater).
      
      
      HVAC ratesThe airflow is measured at the inlet; 1 ton of refrigeration = 3.517 kW = 12,000 Btu/h.

      Reading the chart, and good habits
      
    
      
      
        
        
      • Lines on the chart. Curves of constant relative humidity (gray, every 10%), the saturation curve (φ = 100%, black), straight lines of constant enthalpy (blue, kJ/kg dry air) and, as in psychplotting.m, dashed guide lines through the saturation curve every 5 °C. Wet-bulb and specific-volume lines can be switched on above the chart.
        • 
        
      • Why W, not φ? Heating at constant W makes φ fall even though no water is removed: the same vapor pressure is a smaller fraction of the saturation pressure. Only a change of W means water was added or removed.
        • 
        
      • Dew point. Cooling at constant W stops being sensible at the dew point. Further cooling condenses water and the state slides down the saturation curve.
        • 
      
      
      
        
        
      • Real coils. Air touching the cold fins reaches the coil-surface state; air that slips past does not. The leaving air is a mixture of the two, so it lies on a straight line toward the coil-surface temperature, not on the saturation curve. The bypass factor BF measures how far from the surface it stops.
        • 
        
      • Evaporative cooling. Liquid water evaporating into the air takes its latent heat from the air, so T falls while h and the wet-bulb temperature stay almost constant. The air can be cooled no lower than its wet-bulb temperature.
        • 
        
      • Assumptions. Steady flow, ideal-gas mixture, no kinetic or potential energy changes, no fan work, no heat loss. Temperatures from 0 to 60 °C, coil surfaces above freezing.

      Try it yourself
      
    
        
      
      1. Load the Lecture 5 example. Confirm W1 ≈ 0.0062 and q ≈ 20.3 kJ/kg dry air. Why does φ drop from 80% to about 23%?
        2. 
      
      2. Choose Sensible cooling and slide the heat removed to its maximum. Read the exit temperature and compare it with the dew point of the inlet air.
        3. 
      
      3. Switch to Cooling & dehumidification and keep increasing the heat removed. At what value of q does water start to condense? How much of q goes into condensing water (the latent part)?
        4. 
      
      4. With the Real coil model, lower the coil-surface temperature by 4 °C while keeping T2 fixed. What happens to the bypass factor and to the condensate?
        5. 
      
      5. Humidify the same winter air once with steam and once with liquid water spray, adding the same amount of water. Explain the different exit temperatures.
        6. 
      
      6. Load Cooling with dehumidification, then reheat. Why does the coil remove more heat than the room needs? Compare the coil load with the reheat.
        7. 
      
      7. Change the pressure from sea level to Denver at the same T and φ. Which properties change, and why?
        8. 
      
      8. Run the MATLAB lines under the step-by-step solution and confirm that Psychrometricsnew.m reproduces the simulator’s numbers.
