      How the simulator works
      
    
      The simulator solves the rating problem: the exchanger is known (its flow arrangement, U and A) and so are both inlet streams, but the outlet temperatures are not. The effectiveness–NTU method finds them directly, without iteration, in five steps. The calculation follows the MATLAB functions HeatExchanger.m and effectiveness.m used in MAE 3524.

      Special cases and good habits
      
    
      
      
        
        
      • Cr = 0. A boiling or condensing stream stays at its saturation temperature, as if its heat capacity rate were infinite. The flow direction of the other stream then no longer matters, and every arrangement collapses onto ε = 1 − e−NTU. Try it with the Phase change option.
        • 
        
      • Cr = 1. The counterflow and N-shell relations become 0/0. Their limits (L’Hôpital’s rule) are used instead, which is why code needs a separate branch for this case.
        • 
        
      • Units. cp is entered in kJ/kg·K but converted to J/kg·K, so C and UA are in W/K and Q̇ is in W: SI base units throughout.
        • 
      
      
      
        
        
      • Cross-check with the LMTD method. The same heat rate must satisfy Q̇ = UA F ΔTlm. The solution panel reports the equivalent correction factor F (F = 1 for parallel flow and counterflow).
        • 
        
      • Assumptions. Steady state, constant cp and U, no heat loss to the surroundings, negligible kinetic and potential energy changes.
        • 
        
      • About the picture. Colors show the local fluid temperature on a fixed scale. The parallel-flow, counterflow and shell-and-tube distributions are analytic solutions of the energy balances; the crossflow core is solved numerically and matched to the ε–NTU heat rate.

      Try it yourself
      
    
        
      
      1. Load the Lecture 11 example. Read ε off the chart, then compare it with the computed value and with the textbook chart (≈ 0.74).
        2. 
      
      2. Give both fluids the same flow rate (Cr = 1) and keep increasing A. What is the highest effectiveness parallel flow can reach? Now switch to counterflow.
        3. 
      
      3. With counterflow selected, find the NTU beyond which doubling the area raises Q̇ by less than 5%. Is NTU ≈ 3 a sensible design limit?
        4. 
      
      4. Turn on Hot fluid condenses and look at the comparison table. Why do all arrangements give the same answer?
        5. 
      
      5. Fouling lowers U. Reduce U by 20% and record how Tc,out and Q̇ change. Where does the operating point move on the ε–NTU chart?
        6. 
      
      6. Find a temperature cross (Tc,out > Th,out) in counterflow. Can parallel flow ever produce one? Explain from the temperature plot.
        7. 
      
      7. Run HeatExchanger.m with the MATLAB call shown under the step-by-step solution and confirm that your code reproduces the simulator’s numbers.
